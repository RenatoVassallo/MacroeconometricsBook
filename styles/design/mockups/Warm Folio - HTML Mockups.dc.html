5.3 The Minnesota prior

A VAR with n = 7 variables and p = 4 lags carries 203 coefficients per equation block, against 100 usable quarterly observations. Write the reduced form as

yt = c + A1yt−1 + ⋯ + Apyt−p + ut,  ut ~ N(0, Σ)
(5.1)

and stack the T observations, so that least squares has the closed form

Y = XB + U,  B̂ = (X′X)−1X′Y
(5.2)

Equation (5.2) is unbiased and useless: with 203 free coefficients the fitted values track the sample and the forecasts oscillate. Shrinkage buys precision at the cost of a little bias, and the prior below is the standard way to spend it (Litterman 1986)Litterman, R. B. (1986). Forecasting with Bayesian vector autoregressions: five years of experience. Journal of Business & Economic Statistics 4(1), 25–38.. Identification of the shocks needs one more restriction.

Assumption 5.1 · Recursive identification
ut = B0−1εt, with B0−1 lower triangular and 𝔼[εtε′t] = In, so that Σ = B0−1B0−1′.
Definition 5.1 · Minnesota prior
Prior mean δi on the own first lag of variable i and zero on all other lag coefficients, with prior variance
Var[(Aℓ)ij] = { (λ1 ⁄ ℓλ₃)² i = j (λ1λ2 σi ⁄ ℓλ₃ σj)² i ≠ j
(5.3)
Intuition
The prior shrinks each equation toward a univariate process: λ1 sets overall tightness, λ2 how much other variables' lags may matter, λ3 how fast distant lags are switched off.
Pitfall
δi = 1 (random walk) suits variables in log levels. For growth rates, set δi to 0 or a small value.1
Algorithm 5.1 · Gibbs sampler, independent Normal-inverse-Wishart prior
01Draw vec(B) | Σ, Y ~ N(b̄, V̄), where V̄ = (V0−1 + Σ−1 ⊗ X′X)−1 and b̄ = V̄[V0−1b0 + (Σ−1 ⊗ X′)vec(Y)].
02Draw Σ | B, Y ~ 𝒥𝒲(S0 + (Y − XB)′(Y − XB), ν0 + T).
Discard the first Sburn draws.

Exercises

Exercise 5.1
Show that when every equation has the same regressors, equation-by-equation OLS equals GLS on the stacked system.
Show solution
GLS weights by Σ−1 ⊗ IT. With a common regressor matrix the Kronecker structure factors out of the normal equations and the Σ terms cancel, leaving (X′X)−1X′Y.
Exercise 5.2
Re-estimate the model of Figure 5.3 with λ1 ∈ {0.05, 0.2, 1.0} and report how the eight-quarter band width changes.
Show solution
Tightening to 0.05 narrows the 95% band by roughly a third and pulls the median toward the sample mean; λ1 = 1.0 returns the OLS path almost exactly.
Exercise 5.3
Verify that the Gibbs draws of Algorithm 5.1 have converged, using the trace plot and the split-chain diagnostic of §5.4.
