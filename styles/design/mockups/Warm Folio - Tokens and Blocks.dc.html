Assumption 5.1 · Recursive identification
ut = B0−1εt, with B0−1 lower triangular and 𝔼[εtε′t] = In, so that Σ = B0−1B0−1′.
Definition 5.1 · Minnesota prior
Prior mean δi on the own first lag of variable i and zero on all other lag coefficients, with prior variance Var[(Aℓ)ij] = (λ1/ℓλ₃)² for i = j.
Result 5.2 Proposition
When every equation has the same regressors, equation-by-equation OLS coincides with GLS on the stacked system Y = XB + U.
Algorithm 5.1 · Gibbs sampler
01Draw vec(B) | Σ, Y ~ N(b̄, V̄), with V̄ = (V0−1 + Σ−1 ⊗ X′X)−1.
02Draw Σ | B, Y ~ 𝒥𝒲(S0 + (Y − XB)′(Y − XB), ν0 + T).
03Discard the first Sburn draws.
Empirical application 5.2
A seven-variable BVAR on Peruvian quarterly data, 1995Q1–2019Q4, forecasting real GDP growth and fiscal revenue eight quarters ahead against a random-walk benchmark.
Intuition
The prior shrinks each equation toward a univariate process: λ1 sets overall tightness, λ2 how much other variables' lags may matter, λ3 how fast distant lags are switched off.
Pitfall
δi = 1 (random walk) suits variables in log levels. For growth rates, set δi to 0 or a small value.
Exercise 5.1
Show that when every equation has the same regressors, equation-by-equation OLS equals GLS on the stacked system.
Show solution
GLS on Y = XB + U weights by Σ−1 ⊗ IT. With a common regressor matrix the Kronecker structure factors out of the normal equations and the Σ terms cancel.
